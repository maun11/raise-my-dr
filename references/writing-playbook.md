# Writing the host post

The post has to be worth publishing on its own. If it wouldn't exist without the
link, don't write it — pick a different partner or a different angle.

## Structure

1. **Open on the specific**, not the category. Not "SEO is important in 2026" but
   "We published 40 posts last year and 3 of them produced every lead."
2. **State the claim early.** One sentence the reader could disagree with.
3. **Evidence.** The number, the client situation, the thing that broke. This is
   the material from the interview questions — it is the reason the post is worth
   reading and it cannot be generated.
4. **The turn.** Where the partner link lives: a source that changed your view, a
   tool you compared against, a piece that made the point better than you could.
5. **What to do about it.** Concrete, three or four steps.
6. **Close on the claim.** No "in conclusion", no summary of what you just said.

## Length

800–1,500 words. Long enough to clear the 600-word floor comfortably and to give
the link room to breathe. Longer isn't better; coverage of the point is.

## What Google's helpful-content guidance actually asks

Three questions the post should survive:

- **Who** wrote it — is there a visible author with a reason to know this?
- **How** was it made — is there first-hand evidence, or is it a summary of other
  summaries?
- **Why** does it exist — to help someone, or to hold a link?

A post that fails the third question is the reason link exchanges get
de-indexed. Answer it before you write.

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

## Voice

Write in the member's own voice using their interview answers. Use their phrasing
and their numbers exactly as given. Restructure and tighten; never invent a
statistic, a client, or an outcome they did not describe.

If they gave you a rough number, keep it rough. "About a third" stays "about a
third" — do not turn it into 34%.

## Formatting

- Subheads every 200–300 words
- Short paragraphs, two to four sentences
- One table or list maximum unless the topic genuinely needs more
- No emoji, no "In today's fast-paced world", no "Let's dive in"
