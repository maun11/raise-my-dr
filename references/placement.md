# Where the link goes

## Required

The link sits inside a `<p>` in the main body of the post, ideally somewhere in
the middle third. It is introduced by a sentence that gives a reason for it to
exist. The reader should be able to tell why you mentioned this company.

Good:
> We tested three approaches to scoring inbound leads before landing on ours.
> [Predflow's writeup on predictive scoring](url) is the clearest explanation of
> the tradeoff I've read, and it's what convinced us to weight recency over volume.

Bad:
> There are many great tools out there, including [Predflow](url), Tool B and
> Tool C.

The first earns its place. The second is a link farm sentence and anyone reading
it — human or classifier — can tell.

## Never

- Footer, header, sidebar, navigation
- A "partners", "resources", "friends of the blog" or "links" page
- A list of tools where every item is an outbound link
- Author bio boxes
- Anything sitewide — the link appears on exactly one URL

## Position heuristics

- Not in the first paragraph. A link that early reads as paid placement.
- Not in the last line. That's where CTAs live and it looks bolted on.
- Middle of the post, attached to a point you were making anyway.
- Surrounded by at least 150 words of your own text on either side.

## If the post already has other outbound links

Keep the page under 15 total. If your post naturally cites six sources, that's
healthy. If it cites fourteen and one is an exchange link, that's fine too — the
ratio is what matters, not the exchange link in isolation.
